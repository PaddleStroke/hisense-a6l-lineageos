### 1a. Decoded CSID counters
The register map is stock `msm_csid_3_5_hwreg.h`, which sdm660 `qcom,csid-v5.0` uses: TOTAL at 0x98, ECC at 0x9c, CRC at 0xa0, TG_CTRL at 0xa8. The t34 dump reads `0xa8 = 00a06436` = TG_CTRL, which confirms the map.

- **STATS_ECC (0x9c) is not one 32-bit counter.** It is two saturating 16-bit halves, and **hi16 + lo16 == STATS_TOTAL exactly** at every unsaturated sample.
  - Example (idiag sof#8): tot 9436, ecc 0x22d50207, so hi 8917 + lo 519 = 9436.
  - The "+4.27e9" delta in the camfix11 SUM is just hi16 reaching 0xFFFF (at about SOF 200). It is not a flood.
- **So every packet the CSID detects is counted as an ECC event.** The two halves are probably "uncorrectable" and "corrected" header ECC, but no public source names them.

| row | CSID SOFs | real frames (sensor FC, 8-bit) | packets seen | per SOF (expected ~2158 / ~1730) | ecc hi / lo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| imx576, all 10 rows | 235-412 | ~225 per 7.5 s (≈ SOF / 1.2) | 88k-129k | **312-379** | 94.5 % / 5.5 % |
| s5k3t1 sdiag / sstock | 1077 / 1032 | 228 / 232 | 34.0k / 34.0k | **31-32** (≈150 per real frame) | 77 % / 23 % |

- **CRC is always 0.** Either no packet ever passes ECC well enough to be CRC-checked, or CRC checking is off.
- **The captured long-packet header (0x7c) and the mapped header (0x74) never latch.** The "unmapped" header is a constant garbage value (`f70effff` for imx576 and `ff09ff06` for s5k3t1, identical in every row). A correct imx576 RAW10 header would be `0f0e102b`, and s5k3t1 `060b402b` (ECC computed). The captured "short" packets are garbage too (`00ff110f`, `000e00ff`).
- **Spurious frame starts:** the CSID counts about 1.2× (imx576) and **4.7×** (s5k3t1) more SOFs than the sensor produces frames.
- The CSID status bits b16/b25/b27-29 on every frame are the error side of the same problem.

**Conclusion:** the byte stream reaching the CSID packet layer is corrupt on **both** CSIPHYs, and it does not respond to any analog/timing knob.
- FS/FE and some payload still get through (t33 colour-bar bytes in DDR), so the lanes are wired and toggling.
- The CSID test generator (inside the CSID, after the RX) passes byte-exact.

### 1b. Stock versus mainline, re-checked against the real stock sources
Sources: LineageOS `msm_csid.c`, `msm_csiphy.c`, `msm_csid_3_5_hwreg.h`, `msm_csiphy_3_5_hwreg.h` and `sdm660-camera.dtsi`, plus vendor `camera_config.xml`.

- **Lane assignment matches.** `camera_config.xml` has LaneAssign **0x4320** for all three sensors, with LaneMask 0x1F and ComboMode 0.
  - `msm_csid_config` translates it through `csid_lane_assign_v3_5 = {0,4,1,2,3}` for PHY lanes 0,2,3,4 (lane 1 = clock), giving **0x3210**. That is `CORE_CTRL_0 = 0x32103`, exactly ours.
  - Lane-permutation, polarity or clock-lane variants therefore have no stock basis. They are not in the default sweep. `LASSIGN=` still works.
- **The CSIPHY D-PHY lane programming matches register for register.**
  - `msm_csiphy_2phase_lane_config`: ctrl5 = 0xD5, ctrl6 = 1, and per lane cfg7/6/8 2/1/3, misc1 0 (clock 4), ctrl15 0xB8, cfg2 8, cfg3 settle, cfg1 0xD7 (clock 0xC0 at 0x700), cfg4 clock 0xA5, cfg5 0x52, cfg9 1, test_imp 0xA.
  - The lane offsets are 0x200·i, with the clock at 0x700.
  - The only difference is the IRQ masks ctrl11-21, which are diagnostic.
  - The CSID register settings were already compared in camfix11.
- **Clocks differ. This is the new finding.**
  - Stock `qcom,csiphy@c82{4,5,6}000` enables `MMSS_CAMSS_CSIPHYk_CLK` with **`CSIPHY_CLK_SRC` = 200 MHz**, plus `MMSS_CAMSS_CPHY_CSIDk_CLK` and `CSIk_CLK` (`CSIk_CLK_SRC` = 310 MHz). The stock CSID also sets `csiphy_clk_src` 200 MHz.
  - Mainline sdm660 (`csiphy_res_660`, the `sdm630.dtsi` camss node) names **no `csiphyK` clock** and never sets `csiphy_clk_src`.
  - In `mmcc-sdm660.c` the parent of `camss_cphy_csidK_clk` is `camss_csiphyK_clk`, whose parent is `csiphy_clk_src`.
  - For imx576 (PHY0 → CSID0), CSID0's `cphy_csid0` therefore turns on `csiphy0` only as a side effect, at whatever rate the RCG holds (probably XO 19.2 MHz: no consumer ever sets it).
  - For s5k3t1 (**PHY2 → CSID1**), CSID1 enables `cphy_csid1`, whose parent is `csiphy1`, so **`camss_csiphy2_clk` is never enabled at all**. Also, `csi2_clk_src` (the parent of `csi2_phy`) never gets its 310 MHz.
- **These clocks fit t34.**
  - The CSIPHY digital lane logic (deserialized bytes → CSID) is clocked from `csiphyK`. If it is too slow or off, bytes are dropped or duplicated. That produces random headers (every packet an ECC event), about 15 % of the packets detected, spurious FS, and a few payload fragments.
  - The sensor clocks have no effect on it, and the TG is unaffected.
  - The front camera, whose PHY clock is fully off, is worse (4.7× SOFs, 31 packets per SOF) than the rear, whose PHY clock is enabled but not rate-set.
- **The CSID rails** are the stock pm660 l1 / pm660l l1 LDOs. Stock sets 1.2 V / 0.925 V, but mainline only enables them, so they sit at the RPM default (pm660l l1 range 0.8-0.925 V). This is a second, cheaper suspect.

### 1c. Why the TG row crashed the phone (analysis, not proven)
- **t33 (camfix10)** ran TG rows after sensor rows without trouble (`tpgslow` after `diag`/`contig`). "TG after sensor rows" alone is therefore not sufficient.
- **New in t34 before row 13:**
  - the front camera (CSIPHY2/CSID1, rows 4-5)
  - the stock CSID IRQ mask with its 4000-IRQ cap (rows 3 and 5)
  - the v11 per-SOF register reads
- **A real hazard found in the log:** in row 6 the camfix10 `A6L_V10 START … csid1 polled` shows that the poll pointer still pointed at **CSID1 (powered off after row 5)** when the next stream started (an imx576 row on CSID0).
  - `A6L_V10 START` calls `a6l_v10_poll_csid()`, which is an MMIO read and write-clear on the stale CSID. On MSM an access to an unclocked block can hang the bus, which leads to a watchdog reset.
  - Row 6 survived this once. It is the most plausible whole-phone-reset mechanism in the camfix code, and it is fixed now (`a6l_v12` bit4, default on).
- **In TG mode the CSID keeps CORE_CTRL_0/1 = `00032103`/`00000009` from the previous sensor stream.** So the RX stays attached to PHY0 while the sensor streams garbage into it. Stock TG mode runs with the post-reset values. camfix12 bit3 (TG isolation) writes 0 to both.
- **Safe sweep:** the default camfix12 sweep has **no TG row**. The VFE path is proven byte-exact since t33. `MODE=sweeptpg` runs the TG alone with bit3+bit4, and only as the first stream after a fresh boot.

## 2. Ranked hypotheses
1. **The CSIPHY digital clock is missing or slow** (csiphyK not enabled or not at 200 MHz; csiK for PHY2) (≈55 %).
   - The fix is `a6l_v12` bit1, default on: for CSIPHY k, `clk_set_rate(cphy_csidK, 200 MHz)` (propagates to `csiphy_clk_src`), `clk_prepare_enable`, and csiK at 310 MHz.
   - Rows `iclk`, `sclk`, `istk`, `iclk14`, `iclkirq`, `sstk`, `iclk269`, `iclk100`. The controls are `inoclk` and `snoclk`.
2. **CSID/PHY rail voltage** (vdd_sec 0.8 V instead of 0.925 V) (≈15 %). Rows `ivreg` and `svreg` (bit2); rail voltages are logged in every row.
3. **CSIPHY clock rate**: some other rate is needed (≈5 %). Rows `iclk269` and `iclk100`.
4. **Sensor-side MIPI timing** (≈10 %): imx576 `0x0808 = 0` (auto D-PHY timing) with `0x0820-0x0823` (REQ_LINK_BIT_RATE) reading 0, and s5k3t1 similar. The tables are stock-verbatim, so this is unlikely unless the stock HAL writes more. Test with `SREGS=` if 1 and 2 fail.
5. **Lane mapping, polarity, 3-phase/combo mode** (≈3 %). Stock is identical, see §1b. Knob: `LASSIGN=`.
6. **Other** (≈12 %), for example a board/flex signal-integrity problem. That is unlikely, because it would not affect both cameras identically.

**Link-frequency settle:** the formula gives 20 at 255 MHz / 269.33 MHz timer, and stock imx576 uses 14. Both are covered (`iclk` vs `iclk14`/`istk`). Settle was proven irrelevant while the PHY clock is wrong.

## 5. Risks
- **bit1** changes a shared RCG (`csiphy_clk_src`) to the stock 200 MHz, and enables `csiphyK`/`cphy_csidK`/`csiK`, as stock does. Everything is released at CSIPHY power-off.
- **bit2** raises the pm660l l1 request to 0.925 V (the stock value), within the regulator's DT range. It is restored at power-off.
- **The default sweep has no test-generator row.** Every row is a normal sensor stream, which t34 ran 12 times without a crash.
- **bit4** only clears two pointers at VFE stop.
